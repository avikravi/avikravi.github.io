30/60/90 Day Plan

A first-90-days plan for the Principal Technical Product Marketing Manager, AI Solutions role, built around HP's local-AI hardware line, the ZGX Nano, the broader GB10/DGX ecosystem, and the incoming RTX Spark line. Written to start proving value on day one, not day thirty.

Prepared for HP Inc. · Principal Technical Product Marketing Manager, AI Solutions · Req #3166789

Day 1 Building, not onboarding
Day 30 Landscape mapped, first hypothesis tested
Day 60 Battlecard, reference architecture, first content shipped
Day 90 Motion owned, roadmap briefed to leadership

Guiding Principles

Five commitments that shape every week of this plan, not just the milestones.

  • Lead with evidence, not opinion. Every position I take gets checked against real hardware, a real open-source model, or a real customer conversation first — the same standard behind the independent ZGX Nano case study on this site.
  • Show value before being asked to. The first deliverable in this plan ships in week one. Trust at the principal level isn't requested, it's earned by demonstrating judgment early and often.
  • Fix the highest-leverage gap first. HP does not yet have formal battlecards or reference architectures for this product line. That's where the first 60 days go, not the easiest wins available.
  • Build systems that outlast me. A tracker, a positioning doc, or a metrics dashboard that only I can update is a liability, not an asset. Everything I ship gets a documented owner and a fixed cadence.
  • Say what I don't know yet, and name the date I'll close it. I don't have hands-on experience with every model or framework in this posting today. This plan says exactly which ones, and exactly when that changes.

Day One: Build While I Learn

HP has flagged early interest from potential customers in oil and gas. I don't wait for a quarter of onboarding to start testing that. In week one, before any formal ramp checklist is finished, I scope and start building against three specific, testable hypotheses for that vertical.

This isn't a new muscle for me. The P66 Vessel Integrity Intelligence prototype already on this site, independent ML corrosion prediction, a fleet risk dashboard, and an ASME/API standards reference, was built the same way: pick a real hypothesis, build the smallest thing that tests it, and let the result argue for itself. These three extend that same approach directly into the energy vertical HP is already looking at.

Hypothesis 01
Field inspection shouldn't need a cloud connection.
Prototype
A local vision-language pipeline on the ZGX Nano that ingests pipeline or wellhead inspection photos and drafts a corrosion or anomaly report fully offline, extending the P66 corrosion-prediction work into a live, on-device demo.
What it proves
The "runs entirely at the edge, no connectivity required" pitch, backed by a working demo instead of a slide.
Hypothesis 02
This data will never go to a cloud LLM.
Prototype
A fully air-gapped RAG pipeline on the ZGX Nano over a synthetic well-log and engineering-document corpus, proving out local retrieval against real document volume and structure.
What it proves
The "your data never leaves the building" claim, verified rather than asserted, for the security and IT teams who actually gate this kind of purchase.
Hypothesis 03
Standards lookup belongs in the design review, not after it.
Prototype
Extend the P66 prototype's ASME/API standards-reference module into a fast, local lookup tool for procurement and safety teams checking a proposed material or component mid-review.
What it proves
A compliance-adjacent use case that's easy for a technical buyer to try in a live demo, not just describe.

The 90-Day Arc

Three phases, each building on the last: learn and prove, then build and ship, then own and scale.

Days 1–30

Learn the Platform, Map the Landscape

Real technical fluency and an honest picture of where HP stands today, running in parallel with the energy hypothesis sprint above, not sequenced after it.

Goals
  • Build hands-on technical fluency with the ZGX Nano and the broader GB10 platform, not just the spec sheet.
  • Produce a first-hand, source-cited map of the competitive landscape across every GB10 OEM and the true architectural alternatives.
  • Meet every internal stakeholder across Product, Engineering, Sales, and Marketing who touches the AI Solutions line.
  • Audit existing positioning, messaging, and sales collateral for gaps.
Key Activities
  • Unbox and run a ZGX Nano end to end. Load and benchmark at least three open-source models across the size spectrum, a small model for a latency baseline, a mid-size model like Llama 3 8B, and a large model near the 128GB memory ceiling, using Ollama for local serving.
  • Independently reproduce the specific technical claims already circulating in public reviews, the memory bandwidth ceiling, idle power draw, and context-length falloff under load, so any messaging I write is checked against real hardware, not just secondary sources.
  • Interview 8 to 10 internal stakeholders across product management, hardware engineering, field sales, and channel partners to map the current GTM motion and where PMM support is missing today.
  • Talk to 5 or more real ZGX Nano users or developer-community members to build a first-hand voice-of-customer baseline.
  • Stand up a living competitive tracker covering every GB10 OEM plus the real architectural alternatives, refreshed on a documented cadence rather than a one-time snapshot.
  • Take at least one of the three Day One energy hypotheses to a working, demo-ready state.
Deliverables
  • A one-page "State of the Local-AI Market" internal briefing comparing HP's position against every GB10 OEM and the AMD/Apple alternatives on price, memory, OS support, and measured performance.
  • A first-hand, documented benchmark log for the ZGX Nano across at least three open-source models.
  • The first working energy-vertical prototype from the Day One sprint, documented and demo-ready.
  • A stakeholder map naming the five people whose sign-off matters most to ship anything in the next 60 days.
Success Metrics
  • 100% of identified key stakeholders interviewed by day 30.
  • First-hand benchmark data published internally for at least three open-source models.
  • At least one energy-vertical prototype demo-ready.
  • Competitive tracker live and covering 100% of known GB10 OEMs plus non-GB10 entrants.
Days 31–60

Build the Assets, Ship the First Wins

With the platform and the landscape mapped, days 31 to 60 close the biggest documented gap, sales enablement, and ship the first piece of technical thought leadership.

Goals
  • Close the sales enablement gap identified in the first 30 days: no formal battlecards or reference architectures exist yet for this line.
  • Ship the first piece of externally credible technical content.
  • Close my own hands-on gaps across the open-model ecosystem, so every future customer conversation is backed by direct experience.
Key Activities
  • Build a full competitive battlecard for the ZGX Nano against every GB10 OEM and AMD's Ryzen AI Halo: price, memory, OS support, thermals and noise, and the specific objections field sales are already hearing, like the plastic chassis at this price point and the lack of a Windows option.
  • Build a documented, repeatable reference architecture for a local RAG pipeline on the ZGX Nano, model serving through Ollama, orchestration through LangChain or LangGraph, so sales engineers and customers start from a proven pattern instead of a blank page.
  • Extend hands-on model coverage to Llama, Mistral, Gemma, and Qwen directly on the ZGX Nano, and evaluate vLLM as a throughput-focused serving alternative to Ollama.
  • Publish the first technical blog post or whitepaper: a benchmarked, fully-cited comparison of the ZGX Nano against at least two direct competitors, held to the same sourcing standard as the independent ZGX Nano case study on this site.
  • Turn the strongest Day One energy prototype into a customer-ready demo script, packaged for the first real oil and gas conversation whenever it lands.
  • Run a structured GTM readiness review with sales and channel partners: walk the new battlecard and reference architecture, gather objections, and revise before wide release.
Deliverables
  • A finished, sales-ready battlecard and reference architecture, reviewed and signed off by both Sales and Product.
  • One published technical blog post or whitepaper with real benchmark data and full citations.
  • A customer-ready energy-vertical demo script and prototype.
Success Metrics
  • Battlecard and reference architecture in active use by at least two sales teams or channel partners.
  • Published content shows measurable engagement against whatever content analytics HP already tracks.
  • Hands-on coverage complete across every open-source model family named in the role: Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Gemma, and Qwen.
Days 61–90

Own the Motion, Scale the Impact

The last 30 days move from shipping individual assets to owning a repeatable motion, one measured in business outcomes and built to run without me in the room.

Goals
  • Move from shipping individual assets to owning a repeatable, cross-functional GTM motion.
  • Show measurable business impact, not just activity.
  • Leave behind a cadence and a system that outlives the 90-day mark.
Key Activities
  • Present a formal go-to-market briefing to cross-functional leadership: current market position, the competitive landscape, the assets shipped in days 31 to 60, and a recommended roadmap for the next two quarters.
  • Stand up the recurring competitive-intelligence and positioning cadence described below, with a named owner.
  • Identify and scope one customer-facing proof point, a named reference customer or a public benchmark result, that sales can point to directly in active deals.
  • Formalize a lightweight, recurring customer and developer advisory input loop so future messaging stays grounded in real usage.
  • Run a retrospective against the original day-1 stakeholder interviews: which gaps closed, which are still open, and what the next 90 days should prioritize.
Deliverables
  • A cross-functional GTM briefing deck presented to leadership, with a recommended roadmap for the next two quarters.
  • A live, recurring competitive-intelligence and positioning cadence with a named owner and a fixed publishing schedule.
  • At least one customer-facing proof point ready for sales to use in active deals.
Success Metrics
  • Leadership sign-off on the recommended next-two-quarter roadmap.
  • Competitive-intelligence cadence running on its documented schedule, independent of day-to-day involvement from me.
  • At least one active deal or customer conversation where a days 31–60 asset was cited as directly influential.

Expanding the Channel: Academia

Universities are both a credibility channel and a talent pipeline, and I have direct, current experience operating in one.

I'm a current Rice University graduate student, a teaching assistant for the Data Visualization course, and I built and delivered 50+ hours of technical curriculum to 500+ engineers across 12 companies at daytum. That's not adjacent experience, it's the exact muscle a university channel requires: knowing how a program actually decides what hardware and what curriculum to adopt, from the inside.

What a Real Academic Channel Looks Like
  • Research hardware access. Place ZGX Nano units with a small number of target AI, ML, and robotics labs in exchange for co-published benchmark research, the same pattern NVIDIA already runs at scale, giving HP independently credible proof points instead of vendor-authored ones.
  • Curriculum partnership. Work with a program like Rice's to build a short reference module on local, on-device AI using the ZGX Nano, positioning HP hardware as a default teaching platform before students ever reach an industry procurement decision.
  • A third, non-commercial signal source. Published academic benchmark papers are a credible, low-bias data source the existing positioning system (below) can track alongside press reviews and real user feedback.
  • Talent pipeline. Principal-level PMM and PM hires increasingly come out of applied AI graduate programs. An early relationship with two or three target universities builds HP's own funnel, not just HP's market presence.

First 90-day step: open a scoped research and benchmark conversation with one or two target programs, starting with Rice given the existing relationship. Not a grant program yet, that's a decision for HP leadership to make with real data in hand.

The Ecosystem: NVIDIA, OEMs, and Channel Partners

An honest read on where HP actually sits in this stack, and what that means for how I'd operate in it.

The Honest Picture
  • HP is one of at least six OEMs, alongside ASUS, Dell, Lenovo, MSI, and Supermicro, shipping hardware built on the same NVIDIA GB10 reference design. The chip is not the differentiator. Chassis quality, support, software polish, and go-to-market execution are, and that's where PMM work actually moves the needle.
  • NVIDIA owns the core silicon roadmap and, as of this year, its own pricing decisions on DGX Spark independent of OEM pricing. HP's own timeline is downstream of NVIDIA's, which means tracking NVIDIA's roadmap and messaging closely isn't optional, it's a leading indicator for HP's own planning.
  • Channel partners are currently under-armed to sell this line with confidence, since no formal battlecards or reference architectures exist yet. Closing that gap in days 31 to 60 is itself a channel-partner relationship investment, not just a content deliverable.
  • Other GB10 OEMs aren't only competitors. A bigger addressable market for "local AI workstations" benefits every OEM building on the category, including HP, even while HP competes for share within it. Category-education content co-developed with NVIDIA is worth exploring on that basis.

First 90-day step: establish a standing quarterly touchpoint with the NVIDIA field and partner team, to get early visibility into roadmap and pricing decisions before they surface as public news, the way this year's price and product changes currently have to be reconstructed after the fact from secondary sources.

Messaging & Positioning as a Living System

Most product marketing teams treat positioning as a document revised twice a year. This year's GB10 landscape alone has seen a $700 price move, a new architectural competitor, a brand split, and a shipping competitor review, all inside about twelve weeks. That pace needs a system, not a document.

This isn't theoretical. The Feedback Scraper, Positive Review Scraper, and Competitor Tracker on the ZGX Nano case study are a working proof of concept for exactly this pattern, already built and running in public. The plan below formalizes that into an internal tool.

01
Scrape Sources
Competitor announcements, pricing pages, reviews, and community sentiment, pulled on a fixed schedule.
→
02
Score & Rank Signal
Rank each item by how much it would actually change a buyer's decision, not just how loud it is.
→
03
Draft Positioning Delta
An agent drafts a proposed messaging or battlecard update, clearly labeled as a draft.
→
04
Human Review & Sign-off
A named reviewer approves, edits, or rejects before anything reaches a customer or partner.
→
05
Publish & Distribute
Push the approved update to sales, channel partners, and content, from one source of truth.

Step 05 feeds back into Step 01 on a fixed cadence, not on an ad hoc basis, the same discipline the public case study already runs on.

Guardrail: every agent-drafted output is explicitly labeled as a draft and requires a named human reviewer before it reaches a customer or partner, the same filter already built into this site's own resume-tailoring tool, which strips unverified content before it reaches a generated document. Target: a working internal version of this system by day 90.

Metrics That Matter

Product marketing work has to hold up in a revenue conversation, not just a content-output conversation. Proposed north star and supporting metrics below, illustrative until real thresholds are set with leadership in the first 30 days.

North Star
Revenue-Qualified Pipeline Influenced
The value of active sales pipeline where a PMM-owned asset, a battlecard, a reference architecture, a benchmark, or a positioning doc, was directly cited by sales or a customer as influential. Ties PMM output to revenue, not just to activity.
Enablement
Sales & Partner Adoption
Share of active sales and channel teams actually using the current battlecard and reference architecture, not just aware they exist.
Speed
Competitive Response Time
Median time from a competitor signal, a price change, a new SKU, a review, appearing publicly to HP's positioning response being published.
Credibility
First-Hand Technical Coverage
Share of the open-model and framework ecosystem named in this role with direct, documented hands-on experience behind it, not secondhand research.
Community
Developer & Academic Engagement
Organic traction on published benchmark content, plus academic citations and developer community mentions, since this line has to win developer mindshare, not just enterprise procurement.

The connection to HP's mission is direct: proving locally-run AI is trustworthy, capable, and worth the investment only matters if it's landing with real customers. Every metric above is a proxy for whether that promise is actually true in the market, not whether marketing activity happened.

Planning in a Market That Moves Weekly

The competitor tracker on this site already shows the pace: a $700 NVIDIA price change, a new AMD entrant, a brand split at Computex, and a shipping competitor review, all inside about twelve weeks. A static annual roadmap doesn't survive contact with that.

I don't have visibility into HP's actual current roadmap yet, that's one of the first 30-day priorities to establish. What follows is the operating cadence I'd bring to keep whatever roadmap exists honest against a fast-moving market, not a proposal to replace it from scratch.

01
Plan
Set priorities for a fixed two-quarter horizon.
→
02
Build
Ship the committed assets and content against that plan.
→
03
Signal Check
Pull fresh competitive and market intelligence from the positioning system above.
→
04
Re-baseline
Adjust the next horizon based on what actually changed, not on a fixed calendar alone.

This cycle repeats every four to six weeks, short enough to catch a market moving this fast, long enough to actually finish what gets committed to.
