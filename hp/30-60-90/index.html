30/60/90 Day Plan

A concrete first-90-days plan for the Principal Technical Product Marketing Manager, AI Solutions role, built around HP's local-AI hardware line, the ZGX Nano, the broader GB10/DGX ecosystem, and the incoming RTX Spark line, and mapped directly to the responsibility areas in the posting.

Prepared for HP Inc. · Principal Technical Product Marketing Manager, AI Solutions · Req #3166789

Guiding Principles

Four commitments that shape every week of this plan, not just the milestones.

  • Ground every claim in first-hand technical work. Before I position or message anything, I run it myself, on real hardware, against real open-source models — the same standard I held myself to building the independent ZGX Nano case study on this site.
  • Fix the highest-leverage gap first. HP does not yet have formal battlecards or reference architectures for this product line. That's where the first 60 days go, not the easiest wins available.
  • Build systems that outlast me. A competitive tracker or a positioning doc that only I can update is a liability. Everything I ship gets a documented owner and a fixed cadence.
  • Let real customers check my work. Marketing claims that a reviewer can debunk in a hands-on test cost more credibility than they're worth. Every claim I make gets checked against a real user or a real benchmark first.
Days 1–30

Learn the Platform, Map the Landscape

The first 30 days are about building real technical fluency and an honest picture of where HP stands today, before writing a single word of external messaging.

Goals
  • Build hands-on technical fluency with the ZGX Nano and the broader GB10 platform, not just the spec sheet.
  • Produce a first-hand, source-cited map of the competitive landscape across every GB10 OEM and the true architectural alternatives.
  • Meet every internal stakeholder across Product, Engineering, Sales, and Marketing who touches the AI Solutions line.
  • Audit existing positioning, messaging, and sales collateral for gaps.
Key Activities
  • Unbox and run a ZGX Nano end to end. Load and benchmark at least three open-source models across the size spectrum, a small model for a latency baseline, a mid-size model like Llama 3 8B, and a large model near the 128GB memory ceiling, using Ollama for local serving.
  • Independently reproduce the specific technical claims already circulating in public reviews, the memory bandwidth ceiling, idle power draw, and context-length falloff under load, so any messaging I write is checked against real hardware, not just secondary sources.
  • Interview 8 to 10 internal stakeholders across product management, hardware engineering, field sales, and channel partners to map the current GTM motion and where PMM support is missing today.
  • Talk to 5 or more real ZGX Nano users or developer-community members to build a first-hand voice-of-customer baseline.
  • Stand up a living competitive tracker covering every GB10 OEM (ASUS, Dell, Lenovo, MSI, Supermicro) plus the real architectural alternatives (AMD's Ryzen AI Halo, Apple's Mac Studio for local LLM use), refreshed on a documented cadence rather than a one-time snapshot.
  • Identify the single highest-leverage messaging gap in market today, the open RTX Spark vs. DGX Spark brand-confusion question is the leading candidate, and draft an initial point of view.
Deliverables
  • A one-page "State of the Local-AI Market" internal briefing comparing HP's position against every GB10 OEM and the AMD/Apple alternatives on price, memory, OS support, and measured performance.
  • A first-hand, documented benchmark log for the ZGX Nano across at least three open-source models.
  • A stakeholder map naming the five people whose sign-off matters most to ship anything in the next 60 days.
Success Metrics
  • 100% of identified key stakeholders interviewed by day 30.
  • First-hand benchmark data published internally for at least three open-source models.
  • Competitive tracker live and covering 100% of known GB10 OEMs plus non-GB10 entrants.
Days 31–60

Build the Assets, Ship the First Wins

With the platform and the landscape mapped, days 31 to 60 go toward closing the biggest documented gap, sales enablement, and shipping the first piece of technical thought leadership.

Goals
  • Close the sales enablement gap identified in the first 30 days: no formal battlecards or reference architectures exist yet for this line.
  • Ship the first piece of externally credible technical content.
  • Close my own hands-on gaps across the open-model ecosystem, so every future customer conversation is backed by direct experience.
Key Activities
  • Build a full competitive battlecard for the ZGX Nano against every GB10 OEM and AMD's Ryzen AI Halo: price, memory, OS support, thermals and noise, and the specific objections field sales are already hearing, like the plastic chassis at this price point and the lack of a Windows option.
  • Build a documented, repeatable reference architecture for a local RAG pipeline on the ZGX Nano, model serving through Ollama, orchestration through LangChain or LangGraph, so sales engineers and customers start from a proven pattern instead of a blank page.
  • Extend hands-on model coverage to Llama, Mistral, Gemma, and Qwen directly on the ZGX Nano, and evaluate vLLM as a throughput-focused serving alternative to Ollama.
  • Publish the first technical blog post or whitepaper: a benchmarked, fully-cited comparison of the ZGX Nano against at least two direct competitors, held to the same sourcing standard as the independent ZGX Nano case study on this site.
  • Run a structured GTM readiness review with sales and channel partners: walk the new battlecard and reference architecture, gather objections, and revise before wide release.
  • Scope a concrete positioning fix for the RTX Spark and DGX Spark brand-confusion risk, with a messaging framework that keeps the two lines clearly differentiated at retail and in the channel.
Deliverables
  • A finished, sales-ready battlecard and reference architecture, reviewed and signed off by both Sales and Product.
  • One published technical blog post or whitepaper with real benchmark data and full citations.
  • A documented RTX Spark vs. DGX Spark positioning framework.
Success Metrics
  • Battlecard and reference architecture in active use by at least two sales teams or channel partners.
  • Published content shows measurable engagement against whatever content analytics HP already tracks.
  • Hands-on coverage complete across every open-source model family named in the role: Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Gemma, and Qwen.
Days 61–90

Own the Motion, Scale the Impact

The last 30 days move from building individual assets to owning a repeatable motion, one measured in business outcomes and built to run without me in the room.

Goals
  • Move from shipping individual assets to owning a repeatable, cross-functional GTM motion.
  • Show measurable business impact, not just activity.
  • Leave behind a cadence and a system that outlives the 90-day mark.
Key Activities
  • Present a formal go-to-market briefing to cross-functional leadership across Product, Engineering, Sales, and Marketing: current market position, the competitive landscape, the assets shipped in days 31 to 60, and a recommended roadmap for the next two quarters.
  • Stand up a recurring competitive-intelligence cadence, a documented biweekly digest with a named owner, so tracking doesn't lapse back into a one-time snapshot after day 90.
  • Identify and scope one customer-facing proof point, a named reference customer or a public benchmark result, that sales can point to directly in active deals.
  • Formalize a lightweight, recurring customer and developer advisory input loop so future messaging stays grounded in real usage instead of drifting toward claims a reviewer could debunk.
  • Run a retrospective against the original day-1 stakeholder interviews: which gaps closed, which are still open, and what the next 90 days should prioritize.
Deliverables
  • A cross-functional GTM briefing deck presented to leadership, with a recommended roadmap for the next two quarters.
  • A live, recurring competitive-intelligence cadence with a named owner and a fixed publishing schedule.
  • At least one customer-facing proof point ready for sales to use in active deals.
Success Metrics
  • Leadership sign-off on the recommended next-two-quarter roadmap.
  • Competitive-intelligence cadence running on its documented schedule, independent of day-to-day involvement from me.
  • At least one active deal or customer conversation where a days 31–60 asset, the battlecard, the reference architecture, or the published benchmark, was cited as directly influential.
